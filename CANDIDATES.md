# Dad Joke Showdown — candidate lines, scored against the kit's judge brief
#
# Judge brief (most important first):
# 1. A dad joke a father would tell at the table, within 200 characters.
# 2. Pun lands cleanly on the first read, no explaining needed.
# 3. Fresh rather than told a thousand times, still inside the dad-joke tradition.
# 4. Tight — every word earns its place, punchline at the end.
# 5. Reads naturally aloud, dinner-table rhythm.
#
# All candidates are original lines written for this entry, 1–200 chars.

## Candidate 1 — CHOSEN
> I told my son the money in our savings account is growing. He asked when we can pick it. I said, 'Son, interest takes time.'
Verdict: STRONGEST. Double pun (interest = money growth / curiosity; "takes time" = needs patience / accrues over time) in an original scenario — a kid treating savings like fruit to pick. Clean first read, father-at-the-table voice, punchline lands last, 124 chars.

## Candidate 2
> I told my kids I'm a professional procrastinator. I'll prove it tomorrow.
Verdict: Tight and clean, reads aloud beautifully, but the procrastinate-tomorrow turn is a familiar structure — loses on freshness to Candidate 1.

## Candidate 3
> I told my son the vacuum is my favourite appliance. It really sucks up to me.
Verdict: Clean double meaning (suction / flattery), very dad, very tight. Slightly weaker than Candidate 1: the "sucks" vacuum gag is a well-worn dad-joke lane.

## Candidate 4
> I told my kids the fridge is the most honest appliance we own. It never lies, it just chills.
Verdict: Fresh angle and a likeable turn on "chills", but the pun is softer than Candidate 1's — "chills" is a mood, not a second meaning that reframes the setup.

## Candidate 5
> I bought my wife a vacuum for her birthday. She said, 'It sucks.' I said, 'That's the whole point.'
Verdict: Good delivery and a clean turn, but the vacuum-"sucks" joke is told a thousand times; loses on freshness.

## Candidate 6
> I told my daughter the stairs are always up to something.
Verdict: Weakest. The stairs-"up to something" pun is a known classic; short and tight, but nothing fresh and the setup barely earns the punchline.
